# Assembly

Well done - by now you should have all the parts. This is the most exciting stage, and it needs extra attention. The new upgrades made the assembly shorter and easier than ever: the Blind Joints frame saves up to 70% of the time, and the Metal Kit saves a ton of printing hours and installs much more easily. The steps below work whether you have the upgrades or not.

## Optional components

### Reinforcement for taller Z builds

If you build taller than the standard height, add a 496 mm reinforcing extrusion at the top, just below the front tensioners, as in the picture. It keeps the frame stable even on tall builds.

![Frame reinforcement](../assets/img/reinforcement-402b0a1e.jpg){ width="520" }

### Air scrubbers - HEPA + activated carbon

The project has a small and a large air scrubber. Printing releases particles into the air we breathe, so filtering them is a good idea.

![Big air scrubber v3](../assets/img/blv-mgn-cube-air-scrubber-big-v3-47c4f7a1.jpg)

Mount the fan so it pushes the dirty air into the filter: the air passes through the activated carbon filter first, then the HEPA filter. Check the filters once a month and replace them when needed.

![Air scrubber airflow](../assets/img/air-scrubber-39eddef0.jpg){ width="520" }

### Magnetic heated bed

The most recommended bed is an aluminum MIC6 plate with an AC silicone heater. It has two problems:

1. High price - both the MIC6 plate and the silicone heater are relatively expensive.
2. Risk of electric shock if it isn't installed properly.

So the safest option is a 24V bed - slower than AC heating, but safe. The FYSETC kit comes with a magnetic aluminum heated bed with a BuildTak-like glass surface: flat and easy to handle. The original Blurolls kit comes with a PCB bed, which I recommend less because it tends to warp at high temperatures - message the seller and ask to replace it with his upgraded magnetic metal bed.

These pictures are from the FYSETC kit (see my [FYSETC kit unboxing](https://benlevi8.wixsite.com/blvprojects/post/unboxing-fysetc-blv-mgn-cube-full-kit)). You can also get the bed from the [BOM](bom.md#heated-bed-8-mm-bed). Install the magnetic surface layers exactly in the order shown.

## Internal lights - COB LED strip

After a long time with a regular LED strip, I wanted lighting that is nicer, hidden, needs no printed parts, and shines evenly instead of in spots. The answer: a **COB LED strip**.

A COB strip packs much smaller diodes: about 360 LEDs per meter instead of 60 - six times more - so the light is uniform. The diodes are covered in silicone that acts as a diffuser. The strip is also narrow; I chose 8 mm, which slides right into the extrusion channel. It can be cut every 50 mm, and the brightness can be controlled.

![COB light strip](../assets/img/blv-mgn-cube-cob-light-strip-1-518f9c92.jpg){ width="520" }

My first two strips from different AliExpress stores were disappointing. Then I found a [reliable store](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_9i94Go) with quality COB strips. I chose: 6000K cool white, 24V, 360 LEDs, 2 meters.

1. Cut the strip at the marks (every 50 mm) with scissors.
2. Solder two 26AWG wires - or use [this fast connector](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_9II1fw) (choose "For 8 mm PCB") if you can't solder.
3. Slide the strip into the upper extrusion channels.
4. Connect the wires in one of two ways:
    - Directly to a 24V PSU
    - To a Duet fan output - then you can control the brightness with a slider in the Duet control panel

I use the FYSETC [Duet 3 6HC board](https://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/_A1uWxz), so I defined the lights as a fan output with this line in `config.g`, and connected the strip to the **OUT9** port:

```
M950 F2 C"out9" Q500 M106 P2 S0.2 H-1 C"Lights"
```

I'm very pleased with the result: efficient, good-looking light whose intensity I control from the panel.

## Linear rails - assembly tips

At first I used [cheap Chinese MGN12H rails from AliExpress](http://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/b4ZT5JAu) at $18 each. After buying from 4 different sellers, I found [the best cheap rails at the "CNA Mechanical Parts Store"](http://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/b4ZT5JAu): the block slides smoothly without cleaning. Wonderful rails if you want a cheap solution. For higher quality, the [HIWIN linear rails kit from Blurolls](http://s.click.aliexpress.com/e/cTl59jr2) is not that expensive and very, very good.

!!! danger "Stores to avoid"
    Don't buy rails from **"RDC Official Store"** or **"linkcnc Store"** on AliExpress. linkcnc sold me a used, damaged rail, shorter than ordered - it stayed stuck even after cleaning and lubricating. RDC started shipping MGN blocks in non-standard sizes.

### Cleaning and lubricating

New rails won't be as smooth as they should be: at the factory they are coated with a protective oil that must be cleaned off. Some YouTube videos show how to clean the carriage with WD-40. After cleaning, lubricate the rails and carriages with sewing machine oil or Teflon silicone grease. These are miniature rails, so don't use thick grease. You want them smooth, but not too smooth.

The video below is a good one - just work over a container so you don't lose the steel balls. After reassembling, there will be an empty space of one ball; that's normal.

!yt c53sa46C1qQ | Tuning up cheap Chinese linear bearings

Fixing the MGN rails can be very frustrating: they must be perfectly aligned and straight, or you'll get headaches. Don't start without tons of patience.

!!! warning "The gravity test - no!"
    Don't test the rails with the well known "gravity test", where you tilt the rail and let the carriage slide down. It doesn't tell you if the rail is good: a rail with excessive free play passes it easily - and then fails every print quality test. The rail's tolerance grade determines how much friction the carriage has.
