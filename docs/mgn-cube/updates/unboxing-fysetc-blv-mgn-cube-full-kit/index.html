    Unboxing: the FYSETC BLV mgn Cube full kit¶

    This time they caught me by surprise.

    A friend of mine (@Harel) contacted FYSETC after having difficulty ordering a kit from Blurolls. It turns out someone there took his request very seriously and made a new, truly complete BLV mgn Cube full kit. When he finally got his new kit, he asked me to build the BLV Cube 3D printer for him. I usually don't have free time, but he asked so nicely that I agreed. Less than two hours later he arrived, handed me two packages with a total weight of over 30kg and smiled. Well, he had a reason to smile.

    I waited for the evening, the kids fell asleep, and I could finally sink into what I really love.

    Nothing prepared me for what came next.

    A few moments after I spread the contents of the cartons on the floor, I stood there overwhelmed for a few minutes. I could not believe my eyes and kept mumbling "It's all there..."

    There is no other explanation: someone there actually did their homework, and did it well.

    Someone at FYSETC built the printer down to the last detail, tested it properly, assembled all the right parts, simplified the wiring diagram for easy installation, added an ingenious Neopixel kit to the front panel, and updated and upgraded most of the printer components into an almost perfect kit.

    Without a doubt, this kit can save up to 60% of the printer building time.

    Kudos to those who were responsible for creating the new BLV mgn Cube kit, well done!

    The kit¶

    You can see they cared about everything, starting with the strict packaging. All screws are well labeled, and all wiring is pre-crimped or comes with a plug, ready for installation without any soldering or crimping. The SSR has its own pre-crimped wires, and so do all the other components, even the motors. The motor cables are labeled and their wires are arranged in the correct order, ready to use. They added a carbon sticker for the panels, and each screw hole has a countersink so the sticker will hide it. They added the insulation pads and even an adhesive tape for them. They used the new Blind Joints frame for a quick frame installation and upgraded the power supply to a Mean Well 600W (from 450W). They added installation tools and even made sure the linear rails have red caps that prevent the carriage from falling off during installation. And yes, the rails run smoothly. You can choose an all-metal throat or a PTFE throat. It's all there, and much more.

    The most amazing part is that they took all the hassle involved in installing the Neopixel rings and turned it into an installation of less than a minute. Even my 5-year-old kid could install it in less than a minute. They also replaced the mini IR sensor with a BLTouch and added a BuildTak glass, among many other things.

    I have to stop here.

    It can't be that everything is so perfect. True, not everything is perfect. I wish the extrusions were better packed, since a few of them came slightly scratched. Also, they used the old 5015 fan setup for the hotend, while I have since added an improved cooling part using dual 4010 blowers. The kit also doesn't include the Metal Kit, which is my favorite choice over the printed parts. I have just completed the parts testing phase, including testing the boards and motors, bearing tolerances and rail motion. Overall I am very pleased. Next I will probably build the frame, and as soon as the Metal Kit arrives I will continue building the new BLV mgn Cube 3D printer kit. Right now the kit looks promising and I hope I will not be disappointed. I'm afraid my friend may not get his printer back.

    Before you start reading, here are all the relevant links:

    • The new FYSETC BLV mgn Cube 3D printer kit (cheaper shipping is planned, as they shipped to their US and ES warehouses)
    • The FYSETC BLV GitHub project (firmware, docs and wiring)

    • The BLV mgn Cube Metal Kit (replaces all the printed parts with metal parts)

    The frame¶

    Before I even opened the smaller box, I could guess the frame was in it from the weight and the noise the box made when I lifted it. As soon as I opened the box, I noticed that most of the profiles were protected with a thick layer of nylon, and there was foam to protect the contents of the box. Despite this, a few extrusions were slightly scratched, but these were minor, almost unnoticeable scratches.

    I measured all the profiles and found that they were all very accurate. The drilled holes were accurate but slightly unclean, and I had to clean out some aluminum chips left inside.

    The frame comes as a Blind Joints setup, which allows you to assemble the frame without brackets. It saves over 80% of the assembly time. All it takes is screwing in the provided button screw and sliding the screw head into the channel of the other extrusion, as shown in the video. Also check out our community member Harel's Blind Joints frame assembly video.

    Inside the box I also found a huge plastic bag with lots of small bags inside. Each bag was tagged and contained a certain type of screw. The number of screws was accurate in most cases, and some bags even had spares. I was surprised to find parts I did not include in the original BLV mgn Cube 3D printer BOM, like the couplings (standoffs) for fixing the mainboard to the rear composite panel, not to mention the flat head screws for the outer composite panels.

    Overall I am very pleased with the frame extrusions, even before building the entire frame.

    Mainboard and LCD¶

    As in the original BOM of the project, the kit comes with an excellent clone of the Duet 2 WiFi board, one of the best and most reliable boards running RepRap. Even though the newer Duet 3 is out, the Duet 2 is still one of the most recommended boards. It meets the needs of 3D printers and doesn't fall short of its bigger, newer and more expensive brother in many parameters. It is also worth mentioning that the Duet family is the most documented board family on the internet. The board in the kit is the latest hardware version, v1.04. It came well packed together with all the necessary accessories: a USB cable, an SD card with basic firmware, driver heatsinks, pins, plugs and jumpers.

    The board came with a 5" PanelDue V3.0 touch LCD, an even larger screen than the one in the original BOM. The new 5" screen will not fit the original printed BLV mgn Cube front panel, so FYSETC uploaded all the new files needed to support it. They are available as modified STL files on Thingiverse. I really like that they took it seriously and decided to customize the parts and upload the STLs to Thingiverse.

    BMG geared dual-drive extruder¶

    The extruder in the kit is the BMG geared 1:3 dual-drive extruder. It's a clone of the $80 Bondtech BMG extruder, and to be honest, the clone is way better than the original Bondtech.

    Unlike the original Bondtech, which is made with MJF 3D printing, the clone is produced by plastic injection molding, which is more precise and rigid, not to mention the cheap price. I also checked the hobbed gear wheels, and they align perfectly on the filament. Inside the plastic bag were the tools needed for installation, 2 clips, a PTFE tube and screws.

    Stepper motors¶

    I must admit they did a fantastic job here. The motors were placed inside an EPE foam package, each in its own place, and each motor was labeled to prevent confusion during installation. I also found a nylon bag in the kit with all the motor cables. All cable ends were ready for installation with a suitable plug, which saves a lot of frustration. Each cable was labeled, so you know where it needs to connect, and each cable had the exact length for its axis: the Z and extruder cables were long and the X/Y cables were short, as needed. I was surprised to find out later that even the wire order was correct, so there is no need to swap motor wires to get the right motor directions. Well done, FYSETC!

    I noticed that the kit motors come from a different manufacturer than the one specified in the BOM. I wanted to make sure they matched the specs, but when I searched for data by model number I found nothing. Apparently this is a special production run for FYSETC, so I contacted them and they sent me the motor datasheets. According to the datasheets, they are pretty similar to the stepper motors I originally recommended. Even though I know they tested them on a working BLV Cube 3D printer, I will only have a solid opinion about the motors after building the kit and testing it myself.

    • Extruder: 1 x 42SHDC4038Z-22B
    • Z axis: 2 x 42SHDC6037Z-24B
    • Y/X axis: 2 x 42SHTNC6009Z-24B

    Teflon-coated leadscrews and POM TR8 nuts¶

    I really like this upgrade, mainly because I installed PTFE leadscrews on my Ender 3 V2 prototype (a new project) and they are really great, especially the POM TR8 nut. It's non-stick, heat resistant, and works great without any weird squeaking sounds. The quality is amazing, and you can see it for yourself even though I am not a professional photographer.

    PSU setup¶

    This thing is huge! They managed to provide this 2kg beast, a genuine Mean Well SE-600-24 switching power supply rated at 24V, 25A and 600W (unlike the 450W PSU in the first kit). It's a very high-quality branded PSU that can cover all the BLV printer requirements.

    Note: Before connecting the PSU, check whether it's set to 110V or 230V. The one I got was set to 230V, as it should be. If you live in the USA, switch it to 110V first.

    As you could guess, they added all the necessary cables, including a power cord (with a dedicated hole in the panel), pre-crimped cables, buttons and an SSR module. Everything is easy to install without crimping any cables. Nice!

    Auto leveling sensor: BLTouch¶

    Because the kit has a BuildTak glass bed, the original IR optical sensor from the project's BOM cannot be used. Instead, they included a BLTouch sensor as the auto leveling sensor. It's not the original Antclabs sensor, but in my experience most of the updated clones give reliable results similar to the original BLTouch. It came with an extension cable of the right length.

    Hotend setup¶

    All the V6 hotend parts came bundled in one nylon bag. These parts are included:

    • Fully assembled E3D V6 hotend clone with a silicone sock
    • PTFE throat, already assembled
    • All-metal throat for high-temperature filaments
    • High-quality 0.4mm nozzle
    • Thermal compound, which may be needed if you use the all-metal throat
    • HT-NTC100K thermistor
    • 40W 24V heater

    The hotend looks good and the inner throat surface looks quite smooth. I couldn't see any problem with it, but I would have preferred the heat block to have a polished finish.

    They also included a flexible sleeve (Prusa style) and a PTFE tube with a 1.9mm ID.

    Outer panels and insulation¶

    At first I didn't pay attention to the panels, since I thought, what could be new? Then I picked them up and immediately noticed that they are relatively heavy compared to other composite panels. Composite panels are also called "sandwich panels" or "ACP". These are flat panels made of two thin aluminum sheets bonded to a non-aluminum core. The panels in the kit have relatively thick aluminum sheets, and you can feel it. The panels are installed around the printer frame with screws. FYSETC made countersunk holes and added flat head screws for the panels. The idea is very nice because it allows the carbon sticker (also provided) to hide the screws. They also added screw holes for the board and all the electrical components, including the power plug. Note: Don't forget to peel off the protective layer.

    Another interesting addition, which also appears in the BOM, is the acoustic insulation panels. They added double-sided adhesive tape for the installation.

    Inside a thick cardboard tube I found a roll of quality carbon sticker for the outer panels. They also added a small plastic tool to help with the sticker installation, but because it was inserted into the tube, it warped, so I will have to use my own plastic card. Thank you for adding the carbon sticker.

    Heated bed and glass platform¶

    The most sensitive part is the heated bed. It's a standard 4mm 410x310 aluminum heated bed with a built-in NTC 100K thermistor and silicone power cables. The cables have the right AWG and the correct length, and they are soldered to the bottom pads. The wire ends are pre-crimped and the thermistor wires already have a Duet plug installed. All of the bed cables are protected inside a Prusa-style flexible sleeve. The upper surface of the bed is covered with a protective nylon layer, which must be removed before use.

    Since thin aluminum sheets tend to warp, they included a BuildTak glass branded with the printer name. How cool is that? The glass surface also has a protective nylon layer on top, which must be removed before use. The BuildTak glass ensures a relatively flat bed surface and quick part release after cooling. I'm still considering putting a magnetic surface and a spring steel sheet on the glass.

    The kit also includes a thick adhesive thermal insulation layer that needs to be glued to the bottom of the heated bed, as you can see in the photos.

    You are not mistaken, they really added 4 original Creality heated bed adjustment wheels, as well as the original yellow Creality CR-10 springs for the heated bed.

    Linear rails¶

    The quality of the rails is directly related to the print quality. Although these are not HIWIN rails, I was deeply impressed by their quality. Unfortunately, even though I asked, they didn't reveal the source of the linear rails. I can understand that, since other Chinese stores would want to sell rails of this quality too. Anyway, back to the rails.

    The linear rails came wrapped in sealed plastic bags. I was relieved to discover a red cap at each end of every rail. The cap prevents the carriage from falling off and scattering all the steel balls. Of course, you can remove the red caps later as needed.

    I checked a few things while testing the linear rails:

    • How straight they are. I used a dial indicator and also the simple method of laying the rails on a flat surface and looking for light from below. All the rails were completely straight, with no distortion.
    • Smooth motion. All the rails, without exception, moved surprisingly smoothly. Unlike others, I don't measure how gravity affects the carriage, since that can indicate low tolerance. My indication is a time/force table for each rail, and they all passed.

    Surprisingly, the rails were cut perfectly at the original factory, without the use of an abrasive saw. A clean cut usually indicates that these are new rails rather than second-hand ones.

    The rail channels are very clean, with no sign of over-lubrication, and the rubber seals are clean and new.

    I am definitely going to use them in the printer. Overall, these are excellent quality rails!

    BLV Neopixel controller¶

    I think this was the thing I most wanted to see. The original BLV mgn Cube project translates the printer status into a light signal displayed on the front panel using Neopixel LED rings. But it required knowledge, soldering, flashing an Arduino and very complicated wiring. FYSETC made everything easy. How easy? So easy that my 5-year-old kid could put it all together in less than a minute, and it works right out of the box. How awesome is that!

    Here is what the Neopixel kit contains:

    • 3 x Neopixel rings with a connector pre-soldered on the back
    • 1 x mainboard with an STM chip and an integrated voltage regulator suitable for 24V/12V
    • 3 x Neopixel cables with suitable plugs
    • 1 x data cable for getting the data from the Duet 2 WiFi board (not in the picture)
    • 1 x power cable to feed the controller board

    You should have seen the happiness in my eyes when I connected everything and it just worked. In less than a minute everything was running without problems or glitches. Right after turning on the boards, all 3 Neopixel rings showed an animation and then the printer status. Amazing, and so simple!

    Here you can see the wiring diagram and how to connect the Neopixel system. All the cables are ready to use, with no soldering needed.

    • First, plug the Neopixel cable into the Neopixel ring pins. The black wire should be connected to the "G" (ground) pin.

    • Then connect the 3 Neopixel cables to the bottom left sockets (3 pins).
    • Then connect the data cable (black and white wires) to the bottom right socket (2 pins).
    • Now connect the other end of the data cable (black and white wires) to the PanelDue socket on the Duet, as shown in the picture.
    • Connect the wide LCD cable to the back of the 5" LCD and to the left socket on the Duet.
    • Connect the power cable to the screw terminal (don't forget to trim the wire ends a bit).

    And that's it. As simple as that!

    Tools¶

    Yes, there are finally tools included in the kit as well. Well done!

    LED strips¶

    The kit comes with two 24V white LED strips that should fit perfectly into the original BLV mgn Cube diffusers. I liked that someone devoted time to measuring and preparing the LED strips. It saves us a lot of time, very nice.

    Endstops¶

    Inside the kit you will also find two endstop switches with long cables. Please note that FYSETC made a few changes to the endstop mounts. Download the updated endstop mount STL on Thingiverse.

    Check the wiring diagram to connect the endstops.

    Fans¶

    They included all the necessary 24V fans with the appropriate cable lengths, and each fan cable already has a suitable plug, ready to install. Some of you have noticed that there is a 5015 fan for part cooling. I have since upgraded the original part cooling to dual 4010 blower fans. You can still use the 5015 fan, but the dual 4010 blower fans are much more powerful.

    More parts¶

    They added 6m of reinforced fiberglass GT2 Gates belts, high-quality bearings (excellent tolerance), a few brackets for the frame if needed (front panel extrusion), HEPA filters and even a brass brush to clean the hotend. Personally, I would use the brass brush only with an X nozzle or a hardened steel nozzle. Using a brass brush to clean a brass nozzle damages the nozzle.

    I'm a little exhausted after writing this unboxing review and preparing the pictures. I did not expect to like the FYSETC kit so much. A lot of thought has been put into it. Undoubtedly, the person who made the kit has built the printer and understood exactly what was needed.

    Now I'm left with a huge problem: I don't know how to tell my friend that after I put it together, the printer will stay with me 😉

    I promise to keep you updated during and after the build.
